Methodology & sources

Formulas

Given annual income in home currency (incomeLocal):

  • your_ppp_income = incomeLocal / home.ppp
  • equiv (PPP) = your_ppp_income × dest.ppp — destination currency to live the same
  • fxLocal = incomeLocal × (dest.fx / home.fx) — wire / market convert
  • costPct = (dest.pli_us / home.pli_us − 1) × 100
  • Price level display = round(dest.pli_us × 100) with US = 100

Country data

World Bank WDI: private-consumption PPP (PA.NUS.PRVT.PP) and official FX (PA.NUS.FCRF). Price level index pli_us = ppp / fx relative to the United States. Countries with unreliable PLI (below 0.05 or non-finite) are skipped or warned — never Infinity/NaN.

Commodities

Sample strip from The Economist Big Mac Index (open GitHub CSV) and Numbeo country averages. Gaps are left null. Refresh with scripts/fetch_commodities.py.

What this is not

Not a tax calculator. Not a median / local-class lab. For × median and relative class, see KingIndex.

FAQ

What does PPP mean here?

Private-consumption purchasing-power parity from the World Bank (PA.NUS.PRVT.PP). It converts your home income into an international unit, then into destination currency at destination prices — the amount that roughly buys the same basket.

PPP equivalent vs FX / wire amount?

FX is a bank-style convert at the official rate. PPP adjusts for how expensive the destination is. Cheaper destinations show a lower PPP equivalent than the wire; expensive ones show higher.

Where do sources come from?

Country PPP/FX: World Bank WDI. Commodities: Economist Big Mac Index + Numbeo (illustrative). Net/gross is a label only — taxes are not modeled in v1.
